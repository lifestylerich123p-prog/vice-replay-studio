# Can we use GTA 6 footage? (researched Nov 18, 2026)

**Short answer: yes, the owner's own gameplay can be used, with care.** It's how thousands of GTA channels work.

1. **Rockstar's policy** ("Policy on posting copyrighted Rockstar Games material", support.rockstargames.com, last
   updated Jan 10, 2025; no GTA 6-specific policy as of launch) lets fans post in-game footage for occasional,
   non-commercial use and doesn't object as long as plot isn't intentionally spoiled. Monetized videos are not
   explicitly licensed: big monetized GTA channels have been tolerated for 10+ years, but Take-Two "reserves the right"
   to take material down at any time. Our protection: real commentary/transformation in every video (our voice,
   captions, graphics, an angle), which is also what YouTube's "reused content" rule wants for monetization.
2. **Rockstar takes down**: footage from leaked copies (before release), isolated cutscenes, endings / story
   spoilers, and standalone in-game TV or comedy shows. Never post those.
3. **Music**: the policy doesn't cover in-game radio/score. Licensed songs trigger Content ID claims (revenue goes to
   the label, or the video gets blocked in some countries). Record with radio + music OFF; our synthwave is ours.
4. **YouTube ads (Sept 2026 update for GTA 6)**: graphic violence in the **first 7 seconds** limits ads; after 7-15 s
   it's broadly OK; compilations whose focus is gratuitous violence (e.g. NPC killing montages) can be demonetized.
   The machine now keeps every video's opening calm automatically.
5. **Campaign clips** (streamer footage): allowed only because the campaign grants it, per its rules.

Sources: Rockstar policy (support.rockstargames.com/articles/7bNaeoMFTV0iUDGhStTXvz), Dexerto on YouTube's violence
rules (dexerto.com/gta/youtube-updates-gaming-violence-monetization-rules-ahead-of-gta-6-launch-3405945),
openworlddaily.com/guides/gta-6-footage-streaming-policy, bo3.gg GTA 6 content creator rules,
mubert.com/blog/gta-6-dmca-radio-streaming-content-id. Not legal advice; if Take-Two ever sends a notice, stop and ask.
