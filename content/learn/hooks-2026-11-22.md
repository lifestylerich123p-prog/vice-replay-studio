# Hooks: what gets viewers' attention (investigation, Nov 22, 2026)

Owner: "research hooks to get the viewers' attention" and "continue the investigation on these findings".

## Our own Shorts (YouTube Analytics retention, 20-23 Shorts; small samples, directional)
| Hook feature | Viewers kept (avg % viewed) | Note |
|---|---|---|
| First sentence is a question | 35% (median 130 views, n=4) | vs 60% / 858 views for a statement (n=16) |
| Number in the first on-screen headline | 65% (n=9) | vs 49% without (n=11): biggest hook factor |
| Starts with "GTA 6" | 62% (n=8) | vs 57% |
| Moving clip as the first picture | 62% (n=4) | vs 57% |
| Real gameplay moment (LACY clips) | 89% (n=3) | vs 58% for narrated Shorts: owner gameplay is the biggest lever |

Best openers say the surprising result at once: "Eighteen hours early." (82%), "GTA 6 cheats are real, but there's a
catch nobody expected." (75%), "GTA 6 just got a ninety nine on Metacritic." (66%). Worst: "Can't get into GTA 6?"
(25%), "GTA 6 looking blurry?" (26%), a setup before the point: "Rockstar's boss almost never speaks in public." (47%).

## Market (66 most-viewed GTA 6 Shorts of Nov 20-22, YouTube API, views per hour; confounded by channel size)
- Titles of 40 characters or less: median 95K views/h vs 70K for longer.
- "When ..." / POV situations ("Cuando juegas demasiado GTA 6", "PLAYING GTA 6"): 162K vs 83K (n=4): relatable
  moments, which need owner gameplay (or acting) to make.
- A number in the title (other than "GTA 6"): no difference (86K vs 86K). Questions in titles: no penalty.

## Outside research (2026 guides; mostly vendor blogs, numbers inconsistent)
Claim first, no intro/logo; curiosity gap with a fast payoff; a visual surprise only works with a spoken claim;
healthy swipe-away is under ~25% (check "Viewed vs swiped away" in YouTube Studio per Short).
Sources: virvid.ai first-3-seconds guide, humbleandbrag Shorts benchmarks, opus.pro hook formulas, vidIQ viral hooks.

## Now enforced (studio/vice/viewcheck.py)
- BLOCK: a question as the first sentence; '#'+number in any title (YouTube hashtag bug, caught in the Lucia title).
- WARN: no number in the first headline (unless it opens on a `chart`), title over 45 characters, opener over 14 words.
